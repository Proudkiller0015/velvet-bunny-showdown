## A. In-battle decision making

| # | Principle | Bot rule (testable) | Status | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **[P]** Before choosing a lead, answer two questions at team preview: what of mine is strong into them, and what of theirs is strong into me. The answers tell you which breaker to bring on a free turn, which of your Pokémon must stay healthy for a particular threat, and what to sack. | At preview, build a 6x6 matrix with both directions (damage dealt minus damage taken, speed). Tag each of our Pokémon as **sole answer** when it is the only one of ours that checks a foe threat, and tag **best breakers**. Keep the tags for the whole battle. | partly: `teamOrder()` computes one average lead score per Pokémon and forgets it | new `previewPlan()` in ai.js. Its tags feed `monValue()` and the sack choice |
| A2 | **[P]** A good lead has at least one of three traits: a move with lasting value (hazards, Knock Off, status, screens, weather/terrain ability), a pivot move, or strong immediate breaking power. Setup sweepers and plain cleaners are bad leads. The same species can be a good or bad lead depending on its set. | Lead score = matchup + 20 per trait (hazard, Knock Off/status, pivot, Choice or strong breaker). Subtract a large amount for a setup-sweeper set. | partly: the hazard bonus exists; pivot, Knock Off, status and the setup penalty are missing | `teamOrder()` |
| A3 | **[P]** Guess the opponent's lead by removing their sweepers, then favouring (a) Pokémon with lead traits, (b) Pokémon good into our team, (c) Pokémon that beat our obvious lead. Most games leave 2-4 likely leads. | Weight each foe species by lead traits x matchup into us. Score our lead against that weighted set, not a flat average of all six. | partly: flat average | `teamOrder()` |
| A4 | **[P]** Weigh the lead's risk: think a couple of turns ahead about the case where the guess is wrong. A lead that covers 2 of 4 likely leads can beat one that covers 3 if the uncovered case has no switch-in. Don't lead with the Pokémon you need healthy later for a specific threat. | For each candidate lead and each likely foe lead: if we lose that matchup and no teammate switches in comfortably, subtract a lot. Also subtract when the lead holds a **sole answer** tag (A1). | new | `teamOrder()` |
| A5 | **[P]** Keep the game plan **loose and dynamic**: a few goals ("get hazards up", "bring in the breaker", "keep the wall healthy for their sweeper"), never a script ten turns long. Between events, keep them fixed. | partly: `monValue()` is recomputed every turn from the field, but it has no idea of "answer to X" | ai.js, event hooks in battle state |
| A6 | **[P]** Most turns, make a **middle-ground play**. His six steps: (1) list the opponent's *likely* options and drop the implausible ones; (2) list your own options that do reasonably against many of them; (3) weight their options by probability; (4) check how well each of yours covers theirs; (5) price the case your play does not cover; (6) click. That is not the same as the safe play. In his example, U-turn beats Earthquake: Earthquake is best if the foe stays in, but when it switches it hands them a free entry, while U-turn is fine either way. His Aegislash hard switch looks risky, but the only uncovered case costs a low-value Pokémon. Probabilities shift over repeated turns: after he stayed in once, the read changed. | Search: (a) prune foe replies that are poor for them, like a Sucker Punch that fails, setup into a bad matchup, or a switch-in that loses; (b) weight replies by plausibility, not uniformly; (c) the cost of an uncovered reply is scaled by the value (`monValue`) of what it costs us. | partly: `search.js` blends worst case and average with a fixed `pessimism` of 0.65 over unweighted replies, Stockfish difficulty only | `search.js` reply generation and weights |
| A7 | **[P]** **When not to play middle ground**: when a free play exists (take it); when losing or badly matched (make hard reads); when you have become predictable. When far ahead, just play safe. | Make `pessimism` depend on the evaluation. Well ahead: raise it toward 0.9, so the bot plays not to lose. Clearly behind: lower it toward 0.3, so it plays for the line that can win. | new | `search.js` `choose()` |
| A8 | **[P]** Comebacks. (1) Judge the game state honestly, not emotionally; a bad matchup counts as being behind. (2) When genuinely behind, stop playing middle ground and make hard reads. (3) But read what the opponent will *actually* do, not what you hope. A player who thinks they are winning plays safe, so the comeback comes from calling out their safe plays repeatedly, not from one miracle KO. (4) Play to your outs: pick the move with the best secondary-effect odds when only luck wins (a freeze or paralysis chance beats a crit chance), bluff a set, and play as if the opponent will make the mistake you need. The opponent acts on the state they *perceive*, and they don't know our sets. | (a) When our eval is clearly negative, lower `pessimism` and pick the best reply to their *single most likely* action. (b) Model the opponent's style by *their* apparent standing: when they are ahead, put more weight on their safe replies (switch to the obvious answer, recover). (c) In lost positions, rank moves by the chance they flip the game, counting secondary-effect chance (freeze 10%, paralysis 10-30%, flinch). | new | `search.js`, and move scoring |
| A9 | **[P]** (Rank-1 tips) Avoid hax when you can. His self-admitted losses in the sand stream came from a needless sack and a greedy risk while ahead. | See A10 and A11: when ahead, penalise options whose failure mode loses the game (miss, crit window, extra setup turn). | new | ai.js move scoring |
| A10 | **[P]** Accuracy is part of a move's value. Expected damage of Thunder is below Thunderbolt; don't click an inaccurate move when an accurate one already does the job (he used six Pyro Balls where two were needed). | Multiply every attack's damage and KO probability by its accuracy. When a 100%-accurate move also KOs, prefer it. Only reach for the inaccurate move when it alone KOs or when the bot is behind (A8). | **new**: `ai.js` never reads `move.accuracy` (grep finds no match), so Focus Blast and Aura Sphere score the same | `damageToFoe()` and move scoring in `chooseForSlot()` |
| A11 | **[P]** Setup is not automatic. Don't boost when priority already KOs, when the Pokémon in front hits hard (set up on something passive later), or when the sweeper already wins unboosted, because every extra turn is a chance for a crit or secondary effect. | Run `sweepPotential()` with zero boosts too. If the unboosted sweep already wins, score setup below attacking. | partly: the boost-stacking penalty, the KO bonus and the safety check exist; the "already winning" comparison does not | `statusScore()` setup branch |
| A12 | **[P]** Set hazards early. Hazards pay twice: chip damage, and switching becomes costly for the opponent while it stays free for us. Top players will sack a Pokémon to set or remove hazards. | Scale the Stealth Rock/Spikes score by how many non-Boots foes are still alive and how early the turn is. When our side has 2+ layers or Stealth Rock plus Spikes, and the remover would be revenge-killed anyway, allow a sack to get the removal off. | partly: flat 38 for hazards; removal scales with layers; no sacking to remove | `statusScore()` hazard/removal branches |
| A13 | **[P]** (RMT) Wait until the opponent has set a meaningful amount before using Court Change; one layer of Spikes is rarely worth it. Don't Court Change too early when your remover is under pressure. | Court Change scores 0 below a threshold (at least 2 layers, or Stealth Rock plus one more), then scales. | new: Court Change is not special-cased in `statusScore()` | `statusScore()` |
| A14 | **[P]** **The Art of Sacking.** *When*: low-level players sack too early. If a switch avoids the death, switch, unless the HP another Pokémon would lose is worth more than your least valuable Pokémon's life. *What*: weigh three things: HP; offensive utility **now**, meaning whether it forces progress against what they have left (a Pokémon good into five foes but hard-walled by the sixth is not valuable); and defensive importance (is it the only thing standing in the way of a threat). HP alone is the classic mistake: he sacked a 100% Gholdengo that had lost its Specs and would have been setup fodder, and kept a 15% Moltres. *No wishful sacking*: don't preserve a Pokémon for a scenario the opponent will never allow. *Dead-on-entry is still useful*: a Pokémon that dies to hazards on entry gives a free switch later and can never be setup fodder, so keep it if it costs little. *Exception*: stay in and risk the more valuable active Pokémon when switching to the cheap sack would hand a sweeper a setup turn that can end the game. Elsewhere he adds that offense teams can let their least valuable member die more readily, and that trading to remove the opponent's biggest threat is usually worth it. | (a) Sack only when no switch-in takes the hit for less than the value of the cheapest Pokémon. (b) Sack value = HP x offensive-now x defensive, where offensive-now is 0 when a living foe hard-walls it and we have nothing that wears that wall down; defensive-now is high for a **sole answer** (A1). (c) Never sack into a foe that can set up on the sack (the sack must die this turn or threaten real damage). (d) A Pokémon that dies to hazards on entry is kept as a free-switch token, not sacked early. | partly: `valueRank()`, the TEMPO charges and the never-sack-when-something-walls-it rule exist; no sole-answer protection, no hard-walled test, no setup-fodder check | `monValue()`, `forceSwitch()`, `benchScore()` |
| A15 | **[P]** **Momentum** is holding an advantageous position where every turn pressures the opponent: they have no play that doesn't leave them worse off. It can exist with zero damage dealt. In his example he pivots between Galarian Slowking and Tornadus against Toxapex while holding Future Sight plus Ogerpon-Wellspring. Momentum gets *cashed in* for a concrete gain (a KO), or *wasted*: firing Future Sight as the answer switches in, or pivoting into the passive wall (Corviknight) instead of the breaker that punishes the switch-in. Passive walls like Dondozo are "momentum sinks". He says not to think about momentum explicitly; habit play is the usual way it is wasted. | (a) Pivot target = the bench Pokémon with the best score against the foe's *likely switch-ins*, not against the current foe; penalise passive walls as pivot targets when the foe is not threatening us. (b) Delayed or setup moves (Future Sight, Wish, hazards) score low when the foe's likely reply is to switch to their answer. (c) Keep a pivot cycle going while the foe has no good switch. | partly: pivot moves score a flat 20; `switchPressure()` feeds setup only | `statusScore()` pivot branch, `forceSwitch()` |
| A16 | **[P]** Endgame is the exception to the loose plan. With few Pokémon and turns left, plan exactly: "if they bring X, I do Y; if they bring Z, I sack W". Find the guaranteed or most likely route to a win. | With 3 or fewer Pokémon left on each side (or 4 total), switch from the one-turn search to a small exhaustive tree (depth 3-4, known sets) and pick the line with the highest win share. | new: the search is one turn deep by design | `search.js` (endgame mode) |
| A17 | **[P]** Tera: save it for the win condition. The threat of a Tera still in reserve limits what the opponent can afford to lose, so spending it gives that away. When sweeping, Tera as late as possible. Don't Tera by reflex. An early Tera on the breaker is right against balance/stall. | Designate the team's Tera holder at build time (B14). Outside that Pokémon, Tera only to save a Pokémon that would otherwise die this turn. | partly: `teraWorthIt()` requires a material change this turn; it has no designated holder | `teraWorthIt()` plus team metadata |
| A18 | **[P]** Picking a damaging move by super effectiveness is a trap: a strong STAB often out-damages a weaker super-effective move (130 BP STAB Overheat beats Dazzling Gleam into Ting-Lu). | Always rank by calculated damage. | covered: every attack is scored by `@smogon/calc` | none |
| A19 | **[P]** Habit play and autopilot: repeating the "usual" switch-in makes you readable. His example is Hatterene with Magic Bounce into an expected Stealth Rock instead of the automatic Moltres. | Stockfish/Champion have `noise: 0`. When the top two options are within a few points, pick between them at random (a mixed strategy) instead of always taking the argmax. | new | `chooseForSlot()` tie-break |
| A20 | **[P]** (Latias RMT) Don't set both screens when one does the job; watch for the opponent timing setup to the turn screens run out. Use Wish *before* the critical-HP turn. | Screens: score the second screen low unless the foe team is mixed. Wish: score at 70% HP or below, not only when critical. | partly (screens fail-checked only) | `statusScore()` |
| A21 | **[P]** Self-drop moves: clicking Draco Meteor and then switching out to reset the drop is normal. Choice-locked into a weak move, switch. | Covered by the drop-reset switch and the choice-lock switch. | covered: `drained`, `crippled`, `choiceLocked`, and the `dropOpensKo` penalty | none |
| A22 | **[P]** Sucker Punch and Thunderclap fail into status or setup moves; players read it. | Covered. | covered | `chooseForSlot()` |
| A23 | **[P]** Against stall, be patient: chip with pivots and hazards, watch PP (Draco Meteor has 8 PP), and remove the wall's Boots with Knock Off before the breaker goes in. | Against a team classified as stall at preview: raise the Knock Off score on a Boots or Leftovers holder, and raise the hazard score. | partly: Knock Off is a flat 22 | `statusScore()`, preview archetype (checklist section 2) |
| A24 | **[P-ch]** Note how the opponent plays when ahead versus behind (Rank-1 tips). | Opponent model: track this opponent's switch rate by game state and feed it into the reply weights. | new (low priority) | `search.js` |

| B1 | **[P]** Start from a **progress-making (breaking) core**, not a defensive one. If the seed is a wall, the very next pick is a breaker that exploits the Pokémon that wall draws in: list what switches into it (or what it pivots out on) and add a breaker that hits those hard. | B3 | **[P]** **Defensive overlap is bad; offensive overlap is good.** Two bulky members doing the same job waste a slot and usually mean no speed control and no breaking. ## C. Ten new rules worth doing first

In rough order of value against effort:

1. **Accuracy in move value** (A10/B12). `ai.js` ignores accuracy entirely. Multiply damage and KO chance by
   accuracy, and prefer the accurate KO. Small change, frequent effect.
2. **Sacking like a pro** (A1/A14). Sack value = HP x offensive-now (0 if a living foe hard-walls it) x
   defensive-now (high for the sole answer to a live threat). Never sack onto a setup turn, and keep a
   Pokémon that dies to hazards on entry as a free-switch token.
3. **Lead choice by lead traits and worst case** (A2-A4). Weight likely foe leads, give pivot/Knock Off/status
   bonuses, penalise setup-sweeper leads, and subtract for an uncovered bad case.
4. **Middle-ground search, tuned by game state** (A6-A8). Weight foe replies by how plausible they are
   (prune the silly ones), price the uncovered reply by what it costs us, raise pessimism when ahead, and
   call out their single most likely safe play when behind.
5. **Don't set up when already winning** (A11). Compare unboosted and boosted `sweepPotential`.
6. **Endgame tree search** (A16). Exhaustive at 3v3 or fewer with known sets.
7. **Immediate power, required** (B2). A non-setup Choice/LO breaker on balance and bulky offense.
8. **Breaking core first** (B1/B18). Seed plus breaker before walls; for a wall seed, add a breaker into its
   switch-ins.
9. **The five missing checklist boxes** (B6): Toxic Spikes absorber, Steel type, Knock Off user and absorber,
   contact punisher (soft), plus separate Ground and Electric immunities (B4).
10. **Passivity and pacing tags** (B8, his #1 mistake), together with item and set hygiene (B10/B7/B15):
    don't put passive or slow sets on HO, or one-shot sets on balance; drop trap items from the fallback
    list; slow pivots get minimum Speed.
11. (Runner-up) **Momentum-aware pivot target** (A15): pivot to the Pokémon that beats the foe's likely
    switch-in, not the passive wall, and don't fire Future Sight or Wish into a likely switch.

### Ten rules worth doing first (Round 2)

1. **Breaking core by walls** (R-T1, R-T2, R-T4): pick a breaker with few walls, then add teammates that
   remove each wall (Knock Off plus hazards, pivots, Tera-forcing), checked against stall and matched to
   team longevity. `team-assembler.js` seed phase.
2. **Priority by quality** (R-T10, R-T11): power × reliability × fit, measured by calc against the top
   sweepers with their Teras; make it hard off stall. `team-logic.js analyze()`.
3. **Answers counted in entries** (R-T6): the entries needed scale with archetype (stall 3+, HO 1).
   `teambuilder.js threatCover()`.
4. **Resist and immunity importance from format usage** (R-T7, R-T8): computed per type, so it follows
   the velvet balance patches automatically; Tera immunities count only for rare threats.
   `team-logic.js` rules 4 and 21.
5. **Custom Pokémon viability** (R-T27): effective BP after ability multipliers, recovery, new
   immunities, speed after a boost. `role-sets.js`, `data/velvet`.
6. **Choice suitability** (R-T12): easy-click coverage, pivot, Scarf speed benchmark; not on HO.
   `role-sets.js itemFor()`.
7. **Frail members need support** (R-T19) and **balance consistency** (R-T13). `team-logic.js`.
8. **Knock Off user must beat the absorbers; single-use removal is not enough for bulk** (R-T16, R-T17).
   `addKnockOff()`, rule 2.
9. **Preview edge sets early risk, and ties to Tera** (R-B4, R-B5, R-B6). `search.js choose()`,
   `teraWorthIt()`.
10. **Preserve sweepers, spend breakers and walls; mark the useless member as fodder** (R-B1, R-B3).
    `ai.js monValue()`.
