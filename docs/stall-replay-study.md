# How strong humans play stall (Gen 9 National Dex OU), measured from replays

Studied 2026-09-24 for the stockfish battle AI. Script: `scripts/stall-replays.js`.

```
node scripts/stall-replays.js --fetch --max 300   # polite download (<= ~1.7 req/s), cached in data/stall-replays/raw/
node scripts/stall-replays.js --round 0 --write   # recompute; rewrites the generated block below
```

**Data.** 300 public `gen9nationaldex` ladder replays, taken from the top of the rating-sorted replay search
(the search returned 3000+ games at 1700+, 1644 after the Nov 2024 Tera ban; post-ban games were taken
first, by rating). A side counts as stall when 4+ of its 6 team-preview Pokémon are known walls (the list is
in the script). That gave **63 stall sides in 56 games, by 34 players, rated 1849-1934 (median 1867)**, all after
the Tera ban; 37 of the 63 have 5+ walls, the rest are semi-stall. No tournament replays were needed:
tournament games are unrated and don't appear in the rating sort, and the ladder sample passed the 40-game
target. The bot side is round 0 of the stall study (`data/stall-study/round-0/logs`, 100 games, the player
named `Stall`).

**How to read the numbers.**
- A *decision* is a side's choice for a turn: a voluntary switch (a switch before any move of the turn;
  Pursuit is handled), a move, or `cant`. HP is the HP% at the start of the turn.
- A *threat* is a foe that is boosted, has already hit this side for 40%+ in one hit, or has KOed one of its
  Pokémon.
- A *nothing* turn is a move that changed nothing on the board: Protect, a move that failed, missed or hit
  an immunity or a Protect, or a heal at 97%+.
- A *sack* is a Pokémon that stayed in below 70% HP with a teammate left and fainted to a direct hit that turn.
  The replay can't show what a Pokémon picked if it was KOed before moving, so this is an upper bound on
  deliberate sacks. It counts for both sides the same way.
- Humans play Smogon PP (recovery moves 5/8). This server gives recovery 10/16 PP, so PP wars favour stall
  more here than in these games.

## What the numbers say

1. **Humans switch far more, and much earlier.** 42% of human stall decisions are switches (bot: 20%). The
   median human switches out at **94% HP** (p25 74%). The bot switches out at a median of 49%. Humans pivot
   on the matchup before taking damage; the bot waits until it has already been hit.
2. **Humans heal a lot, in a 45-65% band.** There were 243 heals in 63 human games (3.9 per game) against 25 in
   100 bot games (0.25 per game). The human median heal is at 57% HP (p25 44, p75 65), the same against
   threats and non-threats. Only 1% of human heals are at 95%+ (bot: 12%).
3. **Humans don't let walls die.** 2.0 own faints per game (bot 5.2), and the first faint comes at turn 19
   (bot turn 7). At 35% HP or less with a teammate left, humans switch, heal or Protect 87% of the time (bot
   50%). Sacks: 0.9 per game (bot 3.0).
4. **Status is aimed.** 82% of human burns go into physical attackers. Humans status an already-statused
   foe 16% of the time (bot 28%) and a type-immune foe 1% of the time (bot 12%).
5. **Hazards come later and removal is regular.** Humans set Stealth Rock in 56% of games, at a median of
   turn 8 (bot: turn 3). Spikes go up in only 30% of games, but to 1.7 layers on average (bot 1.1). Humans
   remove hazards 1.2 times per game, at a median of turn 29, once about one layer is up (bot 0.1 per game).
   Humans still have hazards on their own side for 38% of turns: with Boots, they tolerate them.
6. **Against a boosted foe, humans switch or heal; they rarely phaze.** Humans switch (23%, of which 6% into
   Unaware), attack (34%, usually with the answer already in) or heal (15%). They phaze or Haze only 2% of
   the time. The bot attacks 57% of the time and heals 1%.
7. **Protect is for scouting and residual.** It is 10.6% of human move turns: 42% on the first turn against a
   new foe (scouting), 42% to take a Toxic or Leftovers tick, and 11% to receive a Wish. Humans double
   Protect 3% of the time (bot 13%). Wish is used at a median of 82% HP and passed to a teammate 40% of the
   time (bot 14%).
8. **Long games are won by pivoting, not by attacking.** After turn 50, 53% of human turns are switches and
   22% attacks. In the last 20 turns of human wins over 50 turns it is still 37% switches and 25% attacks. 12
   of the 33 foe KOs after turn 50 are indirect (status, hazards, recoil). Humans won 50% of their 24 games
   that went past turn 50. Draws: 1 game (100 turns, a stall mirror), 3% of stall sides. The bot has no draws.
   In the 2 bot games over 100 turns, 51% of its turns after turn 50 did nothing (humans: 10%).
9. **PP.** There was no Struggle in any human game. A foe used up a move's full PP in only 3% of games (Z-moves
   excluded; not counting Pressure). The most any one human wall used a single recovery move in a game
   was a median of 2 (p75 4). Even with Smogon's 8 PP, PP ran out only at the margins.

## The "doing nothing" niche

Humans spend 11% of decisions on turns that change nothing. 55% of those are Protect with a clear purpose
(scouting or a residual tick). 30% are moves that failed, missed or hit an immunity: these are mistakes or
mispredictions, not strategy. 10% are heals at full HP, and 5% are moves blocked by a foe's Protect. A
deliberate "waste a turn to save the key move's PP" play would sit inside the remaining sliver. PP ran out
in almost no game (no Struggle anywhere; a foe exhausted a move in 3% of games), so **the niche is well under
1% of turns**. The practical rule: allow a no-effect turn only when (a) it is a purposeful Protect (R9), or
(b) our key move (the recovery or phaze of the sole answer to a living threat) has 3 or fewer PP left, the foe
cannot use the free turn to boost or set hazards, and the alternative would spend that PP. Otherwise a
nothing turn is a lost turn. The bot's 51% nothing rate late in long games is the opposite of what humans
do there.

## Caveats

- These are ladder games at 1849-1934, not tournament games. The sample is 63 stall sides, and some players
  appear several times (34 players).
- "4 of 6 walls" includes semi-stall (26 of the 63 sides have exactly 4 walls).
- Humans play Smogon PP. On this server recovery has twice the PP, so R13 and the nothing-turn niche are even
  looser here.
- The bot's team pool (3 Smogon stall pastes, no Will-O-Wisp users) limits some comparisons, for example
  burns (none for the bot). Its long-game numbers rest on only 9 games past turn 50 and 2 past turn 100.
- The sack count is an upper bound for both sides: a Pokémon KOed before it moved might have chosen
  recovery.
